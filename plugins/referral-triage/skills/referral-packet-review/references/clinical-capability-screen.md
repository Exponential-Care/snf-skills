# Clinical Capability Screen — Special Care Needs Checklist

Use this table on every referral review (Step 2 of the workflow). For each need:
scan the packet for the listed clues, then ask the user the capability question
verbatim or near-verbatim. Mark each identified need in the memo's Clinical fit
table as Yes / No / Unconfirmed. Never mark "Yes" on your own — only the user can
confirm facility capability.

## IV therapy and vascular access

| What to look for in the packet | Capability question for the user |
|---|---|
| "PICC", "midline", "central line", "port", IV antibiotic orders with a stop date, TPN, IV diuretics, IV hydration | Can nursing manage this line type and administer this med on **all shifts, including weekends**? Who does dressing changes and line care? Is TPN in scope? |

Notes: check for a documented stop date and last dose given; open-ended IV courses
are a cost and staffing flag. Vancomycin/aminoglycosides imply lab draws and level
monitoring — confirm lab arrangements.

## Wound care and wound vac (NPWT)

| What to look for in the packet | Capability question for the user |
|---|---|
| Pressure injuries with stage, surgical wounds, "wound vac", "NPWT", dehiscence, ostomy, fistula, wound-care consult notes | Do you have staff competent with this wound type/device on all shifts? Who supplies the NPWT unit and dressings, and is there a wound physician or certified wound nurse available? |

Notes: demand measurements and staging; "wound present" without documentation is
a red flag. Stage 3-4 pressure injuries acquired in-house later are survey-sensitive —
documenting the wound as present on admission starts at the referral packet.

## Dialysis

| What to look for in the packet | Capability question for the user |
|---|---|
| "ESRD", "HD", "PD", hemodialysis schedule (e.g. M/W/F), AV fistula/graft, dialysis catheter, nephrology notes | Is there a dialysis chair available at a nearby center on the patient's schedule, and who arranges/pays transport? For peritoneal dialysis: can staff perform exchanges, or is the patient independent? |

Notes: confirm the outpatient chair **before** accepting — a patient with no chair
is a crisis on day one. Dialysis days disrupt therapy scheduling; note the impact
on rehab goals.

## Ventilator and tracheostomy

| What to look for in the packet | Capability question for the user |
|---|---|
| "trach", "ventilator", "vent-dependent", "BiPAP/CPAP", "high-flow O2", suctioning orders, pulmonology notes, recent decannulation | Is the facility licensed/equipped for vents or trachs at all? Respiratory therapist coverage hours? Backup power and suction at bedside? |

Notes: this is the most common hard-stop capability. Distinguish a healed/capped
trach from an active one, and nocturnal BiPAP for sleep apnea (usually fine) from
respiratory-failure BiPAP dependence (much heavier).

## Behavioral and psychiatric needs

| What to look for in the packet | Capability question for the user |
|---|---|
| Psych diagnoses, antipsychotics/mood stabilizers on the med list, sitter or 1:1 orders, restraint mention, elopement, aggression, suicidal ideation history, substance use, psych consult notes | Can the facility manage this behavior profile — secured unit, staffing for 1:1 if needed, psychiatric/psychology coverage? Any current residents or staffing situations that make this a poor fit right now? |

Notes: behavioral needs are the most under-disclosed item in referral packets —
an antipsychotic on the med list with no psychiatric diagnosis documented is
itself a flag. 1:1 supervision is a major unreimbursed cost; name it in the
financial screen too.

## Bariatric accommodation

| What to look for in the packet | Capability question for the user |
|---|---|
| Documented weight/BMI, "bariatric bed", lift requirements, "max assist x2+" transfers on a large patient | Do you have a bariatric bed/mattress, appropriately rated lift and wheelchair, and doorway/bathroom clearance? Rental lead time if not on site? |

Notes: equipment rental is a cost flag; staffing for safe transfers is a workers'
comp and fall-risk issue. Get the actual weight — "large" in a note is not data.

## Isolation and infection precautions

| What to look for in the packet | Capability question for the user |
|---|---|
| MRSA, VRE, ESBL, CRE, *C. difficile*, COVID-19/influenza status, TB history or rule-out, "contact/droplet/airborne precautions", pending cultures | Do you have a private room or an appropriate cohort available now? For airborne precautions: negative-pressure capability (most SNFs have none — TB rule-out must be completed before transfer)? |

Notes: pending cultures at time of transfer are a classic surprise — put them in
the "request from hospital" list with a demand for final results. Isolation adds
supply cost and blocks a semi-private bed; carry both to the financial screen.

## Tube feeding and nutrition

| What to look for in the packet | Capability question for the user |
|---|---|
| PEG/G-tube/J-tube/NG tube, enteral formula orders, aspiration precautions, dysphagia diet, recent swallow study, significant weight loss | Can nursing manage this tube and pump? Is the specific formula stocked or orderable? SLP available for ongoing dysphagia management? |

Notes: NG tubes in particular are often short-term and unstable — clarify the plan
(PEG placement? removal?). Specialty formulas are a cost flag.

## Oxygen and respiratory (non-vent)

| What to look for in the packet | Capability question for the user |
|---|---|
| O2 flow rate and delivery method, nebulizer schedule, chest PT, recent desaturations | Can you support this flow rate continuously (concentrator limits)? High-flow devices available? |

## Complex medication regimens

| What to look for in the packet | Capability question for the user |
|---|---|
| Anticoagulants requiring INR monitoring, insulin sliding scales, opioid tapers, Parkinson's meds with strict timing, chemotherapy/biologics, methadone/buprenorphine | Can the pharmacy supply every listed med (especially controlled substances and specialty drugs) by admission time? Any med require a program registration or outside clinic (e.g. methadone)? |

Notes: methadone for opioid use disorder generally must come through an opioid
treatment program arrangement — confirm logistics before accepting. Any specialty
or brand-only drug goes on the financial screen's high-cost list.

## Lines, drains, and devices (catch-all)

| What to look for in the packet | Capability question for the user |
|---|---|
| Foley/suprapubic catheter, nephrostomy, biliary drain, chest tube, external fixator, halo, VP shunt, pain pump, LVAD, pacemaker/AICD checks | Does nursing have current competency for this specific device? Which ones require a specialty clinic follow-up, and is transport arranged? |

Notes: chest tubes and LVADs are hard stops for most SNFs. Anything with a
required follow-up clinic creates transport cost and scheduling burden.

## Therapy and functional status

| What to look for in the packet | Capability question for the user |
|---|---|
| PT/OT/SLP eval notes, assist levels (min/mod/max, x1/x2), prior level of function, weight-bearing status, precautions (hip, sternal, spinal) | Can therapy deliver the anticipated intensity? Any equipment needs (tilt table, stander)? For non-weight-bearing patients: is the facility comfortable with the longer, lower-intensity course? |

Notes: prior level of function is the best predictor of rehab potential — if it
is missing, request it. A "max assist x2" patient who was independent two weeks
ago is a very different referral from one at that level for a year.

## Age and special populations

| What to look for in the packet | Capability question for the user |
|---|---|
| Age under ~55, developmental disability, guardianship/conservatorship, forensic/justice involvement, homelessness noted in social work notes | Does this population fit the facility's community and any state screening requirements (e.g. PASRR for mental illness or intellectual disability)? Is there a viable discharge plan? |

Notes: PASRR (Preadmission Screening and Resident Review) is federally required
before admission when serious mental illness or intellectual disability is
suspected — verify state-specific process. "No discharge plan" patients risk
becoming long-stay without a payer; raise this in risks.
